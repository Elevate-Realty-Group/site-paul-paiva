Central Edmonton Homes for Sale

Central Edmonton is the city's core and the older neighbourhoods around it, from the North Saskatchewan River valley north to Yellowhead Trail, between Groat Road on the west and, roughly, Fort Road on the east. It takes in Downtown and the ICE District, Wîhkwêntôwin (formerly Oliver), Boyle Street, McCauley and the river-flat communities of Rossdale and Riverdale, then the north-central neighbourhoods along 118 Avenue, Kingsway and 124 Street. Most of the city's LRT lines meet here, along with MacEwan University, NorQuest College, the Royal Alexandra Hospital and the Legislature. Homes range from downtown apartment condos to older houses and infill in Westmount and Alberta Avenue.

Buying a home in Central Edmonton

On the MLS® system, Central Edmonton is two groups of zones. Zones 12 and 13 are the core: Downtown, Wîhkwêntôwin, Boyle Street, Central McDougall, McCauley, Rossdale and Riverdale. Zones 5 and 8 are north central: Alberta Avenue, Blatchford, Delton, Eastwood, Elmwood Park, Parkdale, Prince Rupert, Queen Mary Park, Spruce Avenue, Westmount and Westwood.

The two halves sell very different homes. The core is mostly apartments: in the 2021 census, 79% of Downtown households and 68% of Wîhkwêntôwin households lived in buildings of five storeys or more. North of 111 Avenue there are more houses, though it varies street by street: in Delton, close to two thirds of homes were single-detached houses in 2021, while in Queen Mary Park most were low-rise apartments.

Today, apartment condos in Central Edmonton mostly list from $109,900 to $489,775, with half under $209,900. Houses mostly list from $224,900 to $949,000, with half under $360,000. These figures come from the listings on this page and update every day.

Neighbourhoods in Central Edmonton

Downtown and the ICE District

Downtown runs from about 109 Street east to 97 Street, and from 105 Avenue down to the river valley, with Sir Winston Churchill Square at its centre. City Hall, the Legislature, MacEwan University, NorQuest College, the Edmonton Convention Centre and Rogers Place are all here. The ICE District isn't an official neighbourhood: it's the development around Rogers Place, mostly inside Downtown. Today 186 homes are for sale in Downtown, half of them listed under $249,000, mostly apartments.

Wîhkwêntôwin (formerly Oliver)

Wîhkwêntôwin lies just west of Downtown, from about 110 Street to 124 Street and from 105 Avenue south to the river valley, with Jasper Avenue running through it. With 18,695 people in 2021, it has the most residents of any Central Edmonton neighbourhood. The name, Cree for "circle of friends", was gifted by Elder Jerry Saddleback; City Council approved it in February 2024 and it took effect on January 1, 2025, replacing the name of Frank Oliver. Listings may still say Oliver. Most homes are in apartment buildings, high-rise and low-rise. Today 153 homes are for sale in Wîhkwêntôwin (Oliver), half of them listed under $208,777, mostly apartments.

Westmount

Westmount sits between Groat Road and about 121 Street, north of the river valley to 111 Avenue, with the shops and restaurants of 124 Street along its east side. Part of it is the Westmount Architectural Heritage Area, which the City recognized in 1997. Homes range from older houses and newer infill to apartment condos. Today 62 homes are for sale in Westmount, half of them listed under $474,900, mostly apartments.

Central McDougall and Queen Mary Park

North of Downtown, Central McDougall mixes low-rise apartments with offices, schools and the Royal Alexandra Hospital, and Victoria School of the Arts is here. Queen Mary Park, to its west, is mostly low-rise apartment buildings (84% of homes in 2021) and home to St. Joseph Catholic High School. Today 23 homes are for sale in Central McDougall, half of them listed under $173,900, mostly apartments. Today 44 homes are for sale in Queen Mary Park, half of them listed under $209,900, mostly apartments.

Boyle Street and McCauley

Boyle Street, east of Downtown, was the centre of early Edmonton's trade. McCauley, to its north, is named for Matthew McCauley, the city's first mayor, and grew after a streetcar line arrived in 1908; Commonwealth Stadium is inside its boundary. Most households in both rent their home. Today 24 homes are for sale in Boyle Street, half of them listed under $238,400, mostly apartments. Today 30 homes are for sale in McCauley, half of them listed under $249,900, mostly houses.

Rossdale and Riverdale

Rossdale and Riverdale sit on the flats of the river valley, a few minutes from Downtown. Rossdale held two fur-trade forts in 1802 and Edmonton's exhibition grounds until 1911; its Traditional Burial Grounds and the historic Rossdale Power Plant, which stopped generating in 2008, are part of the City's River Crossing area, where plans allow for up to 2,600 new homes. Riverdale, around Louise McKinney Riverfront Park, once had a lumber mill and brickyard; about two thirds of its households owned their home in 2021. Today 13 homes are for sale in Rossdale, half of them listed under $685,000, mostly houses. Today 14 homes are for sale in Riverdale, half of them listed under $579,900.

Alberta Avenue and the 118 Avenue neighbourhoods

Alberta Avenue, Eastwood, Parkdale, Delton and Elmwood Park are the neighbourhoods along and north of 118 Avenue. Eastwood was subdivided from 1906, and a streetcar ran along 118 Avenue by 1910; Delton keeps its pre-First World War street grid, Elmwood Park has compact houses from the 1940s and 1950s, and Parkdale is mostly low-density single-family homes. On 118 Avenue, the Kaleido Family Arts Festival fills three days each fall and Deep Freeze: A Byzantine Winter Festival runs each January. Today 45 homes are for sale in Alberta Avenue, half of them listed under $284,900, mostly houses.

Spruce Avenue, Prince Rupert, Westwood and Blatchford

Spruce Avenue has Kingsway Mall and the Glenrose Rehabilitation Hospital. Prince Rupert and Westwood are mostly post-war neighbourhoods. Blatchford is being built on the former City Centre Airport, which closed in 2013; the City plans it for up to 30,000 residents, beside NAIT's main campus.

Schools in Central Edmonton

Edmonton Public Schools and Edmonton Catholic Schools both serve the area. A few of their schools here, by neighbourhood:

  • Victoria School of the Arts (public, kindergarten to grade 12, arts and IB programs): Central McDougall
  • Centre High (public, grades 10 to 12): Downtown
  • amiskwaciy Academy (public, grades 7 to 12): Blatchford
  • Wîhkwêntôwin School (public, kindergarten to grade 9) and Holy Child (Catholic, kindergarten to grade 6): Wîhkwêntôwin
  • St. Joseph High (Catholic, grades 10 to 12) and St. Catherine (Catholic, kindergarten to grade 9): Queen Mary Park
  • Westglen (public, kindergarten to grade 6): Westmount
  • Norwood and Delton (public, kindergarten to grade 6): Alberta Avenue
  • St. Alphonsus (Catholic, kindergarten to grade 9): Parkdale

Designated schools change from year to year, so check an address with each school division before you buy for a school.

Getting around

Every LRT line in Edmonton stops in Central Edmonton. The Capital Line stops at Churchill, Central, Bay/Enterprise Square and Corona downtown. The Metro Line runs north through MacEwan and Kingsway/Royal Alex to NAIT/Blatchford Market, which opened in January 2024. The Valley Line Southeast, open since November 2023, starts at 102 Street and stops at Churchill and Quarters on its way to Mill Woods. The Valley Line West will add stops at NorQuest, MacEwan Arts/112 Street, Wîhkwêntôwin/116 Street, Brewery/120 Street and 124 Street; the City expects construction to finish in 2028.

By car, Yellowhead Trail on the north edge is being turned into a freeway, with the City aiming to finish by the end of 2027. 97 Street, Kingsway and Groat Road carry traffic north and west, and the Walterdale and Low Level bridges cross the river from Rossdale.

For the river valley, Louise McKinney Riverfront Park and the 100 Street Funicular connect Downtown to the trails below.

Why buyers choose Central Edmonton

Most buyers here want to be close to work, school or the river valley without relying on a car. MacEwan, NorQuest, the Royal Alexandra Hospital, the Legislature and the downtown offices are all within the area, and the LRT reaches the University of Alberta, NAIT and Mill Woods from here.

It is also a renters' market, which matters if you're buying to rent. In the 2021 census, 74% of Downtown households and 72% of Wîhkwêntôwin households rented, and more than 80% did in Central McDougall and Boyle Street. If you plan to rent out a condo, read the building's bylaws on rentals before you write an offer.

If space matters more than location, the outer areas of the city offer newer houses on bigger lots. Compare them by price on West Edmonton, Northwest Edmonton and Southwest Edmonton, or see what your own home is worth with a free home valuation.

Frequently asked questions about Central Edmonton real estate

What does a home cost in Central Edmonton?

Half of the homes for sale in Central Edmonton today are listed under $259,000, and most are from $128,000 to $699,000. Apartment condos mostly list from $109,900 to $489,775; houses mostly list from $224,900 to $949,000. These figures come from today's MLS® listings and change daily.

Which neighbourhoods are in Central Edmonton?

On the MLS® system, zones 12 and 13 (Downtown, Wîhkwêntôwin, Boyle Street, Central McDougall, McCauley, Rossdale, Riverdale) and zones 5 and 8 (Alberta Avenue, Blatchford, Delton, Eastwood, Elmwood Park, Parkdale, Prince Rupert, Queen Mary Park, Spruce Avenue, Westmount, Westwood). Glenora is part of West Edmonton, and Strathcona and Garneau are south of the river.

Is Oliver the same neighbourhood as Wîhkwêntôwin?

Yes. City Council approved the name Wîhkwêntôwin, Cree for "circle of friends", in February 2024, and it took effect on January 1, 2025. The boundaries didn't change. Some listings and older documents still say Oliver.

Is Central Edmonton good for rental property?

Renting is the norm in the core: in the 2021 census, 74% of Downtown households and 72% of Wîhkwêntôwin households rented, with more than 80% in Central McDougall and Boyle Street. Before you buy a condo to rent out, check the building's bylaws on rentals, its fees and its reserve fund.

What should I check before buying a condo downtown?

Ask for the condo documents: the bylaws, recent board minutes, the reserve fund study and the budget. They show what the fees cover, whether a special assessment is coming and any limits on rentals or pets. There are 184 apartment condos for sale downtown today.

Can you live in Central Edmonton without a car?

The LRT makes it practical for many trips. The Capital, Metro and Valley Line Southeast LRT lines all stop here, connecting Downtown to the University of Alberta, NAIT, Clareview, Century Park and Mill Woods. The Valley Line West, through Wîhkwêntôwin and Westmount, is expected to finish construction in 2028.
